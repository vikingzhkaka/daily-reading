当科技成为日常：便利的另一面

In the past two decades, digital technology has quietly reshaped how people work, learn, and connect. Smartphones, once a luxury, are now carried by nearly every student, and online platforms have become the main channel through which we receive news and form opinions. This change happened so fast that few families had time to prepare for it.

The shift, however, is not without cost. Because information now spreads faster than ever, many users find it hard to tell facts from rumors, and the line between useful knowledge and meaningless noise has grown strangely thin. What worries educators most is that heavy screen time may weaken young people's ability to focus.

A recent study shows that those who check their phones more than fifty times a day tend to perform worse in tasks requiring deep thought, largely because their attention is constantly pulled in different directions. The problem is not the device itself, but the habit of using it without a clear purpose.

Not everyone enjoys these benefits equally. While urban teenagers enjoy high-speed networks, many in remote areas still struggle with poor signals, which means the technology gap may slowly widen existing social inequality. Aware of this risk, several governments have launched projects to extend broadband to rural schools.

Yet technology is not the enemy. If used with intention, it can broaden our horizons and lower the cost of learning; for instance, a village student can now attend a city lecture without leaving home. The real challenge ahead is to keep our minds active while letting machines handle the routine, a balance each generation must learn for itself.

Therefore, the answer lies not in rejecting devices but in building better habits. Schools and families should guide the young to use tools wisely, so that convenience serves us rather than rules us. Only then can progress become a friend instead of a master.

中文译文

过去二十年里，数字技术悄然重塑了人们工作、学习与联结的方式。智能手机曾经是奢侈品，如今几乎每个学生都随身带着；在线平台也成了我们接收新闻、形成看法的主要渠道。这种变化发生得太快，少有家庭来得及为之准备。

然而，这一转变并非没有代价。由于信息如今传播得前所未有的快，许多用户难以分辨事实与谣言，有用知识与无意义噪音之间的界线也变得出奇地模糊。最让教育者担忧的，是过长的屏幕时间可能削弱年轻人的专注能力。

一项近期研究表明，那些每天查看手机超过五十次的人，在需要深度思考的任务上往往表现更差，主要因为他们的注意力总被拉向不同方向。问题不在设备本身，而在于无明确目的地使用它的习惯。

并非所有人都能平等享受这些好处。城市青少年享受着高速网络，许多偏远地区的人却仍在为微弱信号所苦，这意味着技术鸿沟可能慢慢拉大既有的社会不平等。意识到这一风险，数个政府已启动项目，把宽带延伸到乡村学校。

不过，技术并非敌人。若有意识地使用，它能拓宽我们的眼界、降低学习的成本；比如，乡村学生如今足不出户便能听城里的讲座。真正的挑战在于，让大脑保持活跃，同时把例行事务交给机器，这是每一代人都必须自己学会的平衡。

因此，答案不在于拒绝设备，而在于养成更好的习惯。学校与家庭应当引导年轻人明智地使用工具，好让便利为我们服务，而非支配我们。唯有如此，进步才能成为朋友，而非主人。
