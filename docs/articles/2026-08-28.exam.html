Green Roofs: How Cities Are Cooling Themselves Down

In recent decades, many large cities have grown noticeably hotter than the countryside that surrounds them, a phenomenon that scientists call the "urban heat island" effect. While temperatures in rural areas fall after sunset, concrete buildings and dark roads in the city keep releasing the heat they stored during the day, which makes summer nights increasingly uncomfortable for the people who live there.

The main cause is not difficult to identify. As urban populations expand, trees are cut down and replaced by parking lots and tall buildings that absorb sunlight instead of reflecting it. Because air conditioners must work harder under such conditions, they consume more electricity, and the extra power frequently comes from plants that burn fossil fuels, worsening the pollution that already troubles the local air.

The impact on public health is far from small. Older residents, who are more sensitive to heat, face a higher risk of heart and breathing problems when temperatures stay high for several days. Local governments, aware of this growing danger, have begun to act before the situation deteriorates further.

A number of cities have turned to green roofs, which are covered with living plants rather than bare material. These roofs not only lower the temperature of a single building but also absorb rainwater that would otherwise flood the streets below. Some communities even encourage residents to grow vegetables on unused balconies, a practice that strengthens both the environment and the ties among neighbors.

Although the initial cost is real, the long-term benefit is clear. If more buildings adopt this approach, the city as a whole will breathe easier, and the summers of the future may become livable once again. The final choice, in the end, belongs to the citizens who decide how their streets are built.

中文译文

近几十年来，许多大城市变得比周边乡村明显更热，科学家将这一现象称为"城市热岛效应"。当乡村地区日落后气温下降时，城市里的混凝土建筑和深色道路却在持续释放白天储存的热量，这让当地居民夏夜越来越难熬。

其主要原因并不难找。随着城市人口扩张，树木被砍伐，取而代之的是停车场和吸纳阳光而非反射阳光的高楼。由于空调在这种环境下必须更卖力地运转，它们消耗更多电力，而额外的电力往往来自燃烧化石燃料的工厂，从而加剧了本已困扰当地空气的污染。

对公众健康的影响绝不算小。对高温更敏感的老年居民，在持续高温数日时面临更高的心脏与呼吸疾病风险。意识到这一日益增长的威胁，地方政府开始在情况进一步恶化前采取行动。

不少城市转向了绿色屋顶——以活体植物而非裸露材料覆盖的屋顶。这些屋顶不仅能降低单栋建筑的温度，还能吸收本会让街道积水的雨水。一些社区甚至鼓励居民在闲置阳台种植蔬菜，这种做法既改善了环境，也强化了邻里之间的联系。

尽管初期投入真实存在，长期收益却很清楚。若更多建筑采用这一方式，整座城市将呼吸得更顺畅，未来的夏天或许会重新变得宜居。最终的选择，说到底，属于那些决定街道如何建造的市民。
