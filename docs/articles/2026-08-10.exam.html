The Science of Sleep

Few habits influence our health as deeply as sleep, yet for a long time it was the least understood. Scientists once believed that a sleeping brain simply switched off, conserving energy until morning. Modern research tells a different story: far from resting, the brain remains remarkably active, sorting memories, repairing cells, and clearing waste that builds up during waking hours.

The cycle of sleep is itself a finely tuned process. Rather than falling into one uniform state, we move through several stages, from light dozing to deep slow-wave sleep and on to REM, the phase in which most dreaming occurs. A full night normally contains four or five of these cycles, each lasting about ninety minutes. It is during the deeper stages that the body releases growth hormone and strengthens the immune system, which is why poor sleep leaves us not only tired but more likely to fall ill.

What happens when sleep is cut short? The costs accumulate quietly. Attention slips, mood turns sour, and the ability to learn new information drops sharply. Adolescents, whose brains are still developing, are especially vulnerable; studies have linked chronic sleep loss among students to weaker academic performance and higher rates of anxiety.

Fortunately, the remedies are neither mysterious nor expensive. Keeping a regular bedtime, dimming screens an hour before sleep, and avoiding caffeine late in the day can restore a healthier rhythm. Even a short afternoon rest, when genuinely needed, helps the body reset without harming the night. Just as important is the cultural shift of treating rest as productive rather than wasteful—a lesson that societies increasingly ignore at their own cost.

In the end, sleep is not the absence of life but a central part of it. Those who respect its rhythm tend to think more clearly, feel more steadily, and meet the day with greater reserve.

中文译文

很少有哪种习惯能像睡眠一样深刻地影响我们的健康，然而在很长一段时间里，它却最不为人所理解。科学家曾认为，睡眠中的大脑只是简单地"关机"，为次日清晨积蓄能量。现代研究讲述的却是另一番景象：大脑远未休息，而是异常活跃——整理记忆、修复细胞，并清除清醒时累积的废物。

睡眠的循环本身是一套精密调校的过程。我们并非陷入某种单一的状态，而是依次经历若干阶段，从浅眠、深度慢波睡眠，一直进入到快速眼动期——大多数梦境就发生在这个阶段。一个完整的夜晚通常包含四到五个这样的周期，每个约九十分钟。正是在较深的阶段，身体才会释放生长激素、增强免疫系统，这也解释了为何睡眠不足不仅令人疲惫，还更容易生病。

当睡眠被压缩时会发生什么？代价是悄然累积的。注意力下滑，情绪转坏，学习新信息的能力急剧下降。大脑尚在发育的青少年尤其脆弱；研究已将学生长期缺觉与较差的学业表现、更高的焦虑水平联系起来。

所幸，对策既非神秘也非昂贵。保持规律的就寝时间、睡前一小时调暗屏幕、白天晚些时候避免咖啡因，都能恢复更健康的节律。同样重要的是一种文化转变——把休息视作有产出的，而非浪费；这个教训，社会正越来越以自身代价将其忽略。

归根结底，睡眠不是生命的缺席，而是生命的核心部分。尊重其节律的人，往往思维更清晰、情绪更平稳，并以更充沛的底气迎接每一天。
