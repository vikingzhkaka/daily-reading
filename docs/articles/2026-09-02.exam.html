When the City Lost Its Shine

In recent years, a quiet but visible shift has taken place across China: a growing number of young graduates, who once rushed to first-tier cities, are now choosing to return to their hometowns or settle in smaller towns.

This reversal is driven by several factors. Because housing prices in megacities have remained high, many newcomers find it increasingly difficult to put down roots. At the same time, remote work and the spread of digital infrastructure have made smaller cities far more livable than they used to be. Whereas the old pattern rewarded only those who endured endless commutes, the new model values balance over mere income.

The impact is twofold. On the one hand, returning youth bring fresh skills, capital, and ideas that help revive local economies. On the other hand, their departure eases the pressure on overcrowded metropolises, though it also leaves some employers short of talent.

In response, local governments have begun offering subsidies, affordable housing, and startup support to attract these returnees. Nevertheless, experts warn that policy alone is not enough; what truly keeps young people is a sense of belonging and real career growth. Only when smaller towns provide both opportunity and community will the trend prove sustainable.

中文译文

近年来，中国出现了一股平静却明显的变化：越来越多曾涌向一线城市的年轻毕业生，如今选择回到家乡或在更小的城镇定居。

这一逆转由多重因素驱动。由于特大城市的房价居高不下，许多新移民越来越难扎根。与此同时，远程办公与数字基础设施的普及，让小城市比过去宜居得多。旧模式只奖励那些忍受无尽通勤的人，而新模式更看重平衡而非单纯的收入。

影响是双面的。一方面，返乡青年带来了新鲜技能、资金和创意，有助于重振地方经济。另一方面，他们的离开缓解了过度拥挤的大都市的压力，尽管这也让部分雇主面临人才短缺。

作为回应，地方政府已开始提供补贴、保障性住房和创业支持，以吸引这些返乡者。然而专家警告，单靠政策还不够；真正留住年轻人的，是归属感和实实在在的职业成长。只有当小城市同时提供机会与社群，这一趋势才具备可持续性。
