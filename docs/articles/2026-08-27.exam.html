The Teacher Who Turned a Bus into a Library

When Daniel Owens retired from teaching at the age of sixty, he did not expect to begin anything new. Yet in the small farming town where he had spent his whole career, one problem had troubled him for decades: the nearest public library stood forty kilometres away, and few families owned a car.

The idea came to him by accident. Walking home one evening, he noticed an abandoned school bus rusting behind a garage. Its engine was beyond repair, but the body was still sound. What if, he wondered, the books could travel to the children instead?

Persuading others proved harder than he had imagined. The town council was reluctant to spend money on what it called a hobby, and several neighbours doubted that children who preferred screens would ever climb aboard. Owens did not argue. Instead, he spent four months removing the seats, fitting wooden shelves along both walls, and repainting the exterior a bright yellow that could be seen from the main road.

Books arrived slowly at first. He wrote to former students, asking each of them to donate a single volume that had mattered to them, together with a short note explaining why. That personal detail proved decisive. Within a year the bus held more than two thousand books, and the handwritten notes tucked inside them became the feature children liked best.

The effect went far beyond reading. Because the bus was parked near the market square, it gradually became a place where teenagers finished their homework and older residents came to read the newspapers. Attendance at the town's evening classes rose, and two volunteers now keep the library open on weekends.

Owens is careful not to claim too much credit. "I did not build anything," he says. "I only moved the books closer." Still, his example suggests something worth remembering: many obstacles that appear financial are, in fact, matters of distance and imagination. A solution need not be expensive in order to be effective; it must simply be within reach.

中文译文

丹尼尔·欧文斯六十岁从教师岗位退休时，并没打算再开始什么新事情。然而，在他倾注了整个职业生涯的那座农业小镇里，有一个问题困扰了他几十年：最近的公共图书馆在四十公里之外，而很少有家庭拥有汽车。

这个念头是偶然冒出来的。一天傍晚走回家时，他注意到一辆废弃的校车锈在一间车库后面。它的发动机已经无法修复，但车身还算完好。他心想：要是让书本自己走到孩子们身边呢？

说服别人比他想象的更难。镇议会不愿意把钱花在它口中的"个人爱好"上，还有几位邻居怀疑：那些更喜欢屏幕的孩子，会愿意上车吗？欧文斯没有争辩。他花了四个月拆掉座椅，沿两侧车壁装上木书架，并把车身重新刷成一种从主路上就能看见的亮黄色。

起初书来得很慢。他写信给从前的学生，请每个人捐出一本对自己有意义的书，并附上一张简短的字条说明理由。正是这个私人化的细节起了决定性作用。不到一年，这辆车里就装了两千多本书，而夹在书页间的手写字条，成了孩子们最喜欢的地方。

它的影响远远超出了阅读本身。由于车停在集市广场附近，这里渐渐变成了少年们写作业、年长居民读报的场所。小镇夜校的出席率上升了，如今还有两位志愿者在周末维持图书馆开放。

欧文斯很谨慎，不愿把功劳都归于自己。"我什么都没建起来，"他说，"我只是把书搬得更近了一点。"不过，他的例子提示了一件值得记住的事：许多看似是钱的障碍，实际上是距离和想象力的问题。一个办法不必昂贵才有效；它只需要伸手可及。
