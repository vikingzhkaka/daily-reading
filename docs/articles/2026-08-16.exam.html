Learning to Be Neighbors Again

In many modern neighborhoods, the person living next door remains a stranger. We share a wall and a postcode, yet our lives rarely touch; a busy schedule and a locked front door keep us apart. This quiet isolation, once an accepted side effect of city life, is now drawing fresh attention as people sense what its loss costs them.

The decline of neighborly contact did not happen by a single decision. Suburbs built for cars left few places to meet on foot; longer working hours shrank the time at home; and screens, though connecting us to distant friends, pulled us away from the people on the same floor. The result is a paradox: more ways to communicate, fewer faces we actually know.

Why does it matter? Because nearby relationships are a kind of insurance. A trusted neighbor can collect a parcel, watch a child for ten minutes, or notice when something is wrong. During crises—a flood, a power cut, a pandemic—communities with strong ties coped far better than those made of strangers. Beyond safety, casual friendliness simply makes a street feel like a place rather than a passage.

Rebuilding the habit is less grand than it sounds. Borrowing a tool, greeting someone by name, or organizing a shared meal on the landing costs little and opens a door. What slows people is not hostility but uncertainty about whether the other side welcomes approach. A small, repeated gesture usually answers that question faster than any plan. Trust, once offered, tends to return in kind.

In the end, a neighborhood is not given; it is made, one ordinary interaction at a time. The stranger next door may simply be a friend the building has not yet introduced.

中文译文

在许多现代社区里，住隔壁的人依然是个陌生人。我们共用一堵墙、同一个邮编，生活却鲜有交集；忙碌的日程和一扇锁上的大门把我们隔开。这种安静的孤立，曾被视为城市生活的必然副作用，如今正引发新的关注——因为人们察觉到了失去它所要付出的代价。

邻里接触的减少并非由某一个决定造成。为汽车而建的郊区，几乎没留下步行相遇的场所；更长的工作时间压缩了在家的时光；而屏幕虽把我们与远方的朋友相连，却把我们拉离了同一层楼的人。结果是一个悖论：沟通的方式更多了，真正认识的面孔却更少了。

这为何要紧？因为就近的关系是某种保险。一位可信的邻居能帮收包裹、照看孩子十分钟，或在情况不对时察觉异样。在危机中——洪水、停电、疫情——纽带紧密的社区，远比由陌生人凑成的社区应对得好。除了安全，随口的友善本身，就让一条街像一个"地方"，而非一条"过道"。

重建这种习惯，没听起来那么宏大。借一件工具、以名字问候、或在楼道里组织一顿共享的饭，花费甚少，却打开了一扇门。让人止步的不是敌意，而是不确定对方是否欢迎靠近。一个微小而重复的举动，通常比任何计划都更快回答这个问题。

归根结底，邻里不是被给予的，而是被建造的——一次寻常的互动接一次。隔壁的陌生人，也许只是这栋楼尚未引见的朋友。
